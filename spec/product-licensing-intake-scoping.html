The failure chain

how a request dies today
01
A request is born late

A product manager, pillar lead, or salesperson needs a new part number, a price change, or a structural change. It typically surfaces at the very end of the product cycle, when quoting, booking, and billing are already waiting on it.

02
It goes to a generic inbox

The request is an email, sometimes with the Word or Excel form attached, sometimes free text. There is no required structure, no request type, and no way to tell a clone request from a new license model.

03
Nobody can triage it

Pricing Operations tracks arrivals manually. There is no queue, no classification, no SLA, and no visibility for the requester. Requests are missed or resurface as escalations.

04
The formal process is skipped

A New Product Release process (v4.0, Promapp and Nintex) exists on paper, with defined roles and systems of record. It has not been consistently followed, and the tooling in use today (Wrike) covers only the final field-notification step, not upstream intake or triggering.

05
The damage compounds downstream

Parts get created without a dedupe check, so the catalog keeps sprawling. Quotes happen without valid part numbers, heaviest where CPQ adoption is weak. Order management inherits rework, and there is nothing defensible to show an auditor.

Why this matters now

SOX readiness

As a newly public company, price and catalog changes need a documented request, approval, and execution trail. Email cannot provide one.

The price is authored once

Every price is authored once in Conga on the price list item; everything downstream reads a copy. The integrity of the request that triggers that authoring step is the whole game.

SKU rationalization

The deprecation cut list only holds if the front door stops uncontrolled creation. Intake governance is the prevention half of rationalization.

Six stages from request to load, with one branch: simple requests move fast, structural requests get reviewed early by the right functions. The design principle is a single front door, complexity-based routing, and a log of every step.
1

One front door

Requester · PM, pillar lead, sales

All requests enter through a single structured form. The generic inbox is retired (it auto-replies with the link), and the two current forms become required fields inside the request. Every request declares one of five types:

Create part numbernew product or new flavor
Change pricelist, maintenance, or transfer
Change attributeslicense model, platform, charge type
Retire / EOLremove from the sellable catalog
Structure changebundle, tier, or offering model
Required fields per request
Requester and function, pillar and solution, business justification, target effective date, affected part numbers (or "new"), proposed price and currency, license model and charge type, and whether an existing part could serve instead. Multi-part requests carry line items rather than an attached spreadsheet.
2

Triage and routing

Pricing Operations · within 2 business days

Operations classifies every request on arrival. The routing rule is the one already agreed in principle:

Fast track

A copy of an existing product with no new variables: clone, price update within policy, or straightforward retirement. Goes directly to the Operations execution queue.

Full review

Anything introducing a new pricing model, tier model, offering structure, or license model. Routes to Pricing Strategy review and triggers the cross-functional relevance check in stage 3.

3

Cross-functional relevance check

Full-review requests only

The request form carries each function's relevance questions, so the right teams are pulled in at intake rather than discovering the product at booking. Only functions whose answers flag relevance are engaged.

LegalFinanceSales OperationsOrder ManagementSoftware DeliveryRevenue RecognitionCloud OperationsIT and ProvisioningSupport
4

Two-step approval

Pricing Strategy → Pricing Operations

Step one: Pricing Strategy signs off on the commercial design (price, model, structure). Step two: Pricing Operations accepts the request as executable (complete, unambiguous, loadable). Approval thresholds align to delegated pricing authority once the committee grants it, so routine requests do not escalate and exceptional ones must.

5

Execute and load

Pricing Operations

The part number is created under taxonomy rules: a dedupe check against the existing catalog first, and a preference for attribute or matrix pricing over a new SKU wherever the system allows it. The price is authored once in Conga on the price list item; downstream systems read from there.

6

Close, notify, log

System of record

The requester receives confirmation with the final part number and effective date, the field is notified, and the request closes with a full timestamped history: who asked, who approved, what changed, when it loaded. That history is the audit trail and feeds the pricing decision log.

Standing guardrails

No part number, no quote

A quote attempt with a missing part routes to deal desk for exception approval, not around the system.

Dedupe before create

No new SKU without checking whether an existing part or an attribute deviation already covers it.

One authoring point

Prices change in Conga on the PLI, through this process, and nowhere else.

Everything logged

No request, approval, or load exists outside the system. Email is not a channel.

The question is not which tool is better in the abstract. It is which tool should carry the production process, and which should be used to design it. The two candidates are good at different halves of the problem.

Recommendation

Design in Claude Code. Run in Salesforce.

These are not competing answers; they are two phases of the same build. The Claude Code clay model is already a committed KR: use it to encode the routing rules, the five request types, the relevance questions, and the approval thresholds, and let Pricing Operations and the pillar leads react to something real. The validated model then becomes the requirements specification for the Salesforce build, which is where the process should live in production: native approvals, native audit history, native queues, and requesters who are already in the system. This sequencing also de-risks the Business IT engagement, because they receive tested requirements instead of a discovery project.

PHASE 1 · NOW
Clay model in Claude Code

Working prototype of intake, triage, routing, and approvals. Pricing Operations and selected requesters test it against real historical requests.

PHASE 2 · AFTER VALIDATION
Salesforce build

Business IT implements the validated design as a request type with a two-step approval flow, an Operations queue, and dashboards. ALI instance first.

PHASE 3 · HANDOFF
Pricing Operations owns it

Queue ownership, triage, and execution transfer to Operations. The generic inbox is retired. Strategy retains the governance rules and taxonomy.

Caveats to confirm

  • Instance fragmentation: three Salesforce instances exist (ALI, SIG, Projectmates). Version one targets ALI only; confirm nothing in the design blocks a later extension.
  • Requester licensing: confirm the likely requesters (product managers, pillar leads, sales) hold licenses that allow them to submit and track requests.
  • No Conga integration in version one: the load into Conga remains a manual Operations step with a confirmation field, which keeps the build small and the audit trail intact.
  • The greenfield CRM and ERP program will eventually replace the platform underneath this. The process design, not the implementation, is the durable asset, which is exactly why the clay model matters.
The standard pattern for new product introduction and catalog-change governance is consistent across mature software companies: one intake, typed requests, complexity-based routing, early cross-functional gates, thresholded approvals, governed execution, and measurement. Reading the scoreboard

Six of ten practices are absent, two are partial, and two are planned and gated on decisions already in flight (delegated authority, the deal-desk rule). None of the gaps requires new invention: every one is closed by the workflow on tab 02 running on the platform decision on tab 03. That is the strongest argument that this is a build-and-adopt problem, not a design problem.

Version one scope

In scope
  • ALI Salesforce instance only
  • Five request types with required fields and line items for multi-part requests
  • Fast-track versus full-review routing at triage
  • Two-step approval (Pricing Strategy, then Pricing Operations)
  • Operations queue, dashboard, and aging report
  • Field history tracking on the request record
  • Requester confirmation on close, with the final part number and effective date
  • Generic inbox retired with an auto-reply pointing to the new intake
Out of scope, v1
  • Conga integration: the PLI load stays a manual Operations step with a confirmation field
  • SIG and Projectmates instances
  • Automated part-number generation from attributes
  • The stage 2 to 3 opportunity trigger (version two, once intake is stable)
  • Replacing the Wrike field-notification step
  • The nine-function relevance questionnaire in full: version one captures the answers as form fields, automation of the routing comes later

Questions for the Salesforce scoping call

in priority order
Object and coexistence

Can a dedicated request type be added beside the existing general-support tickets, and on which object would it live? Does anything about the current setup constrain us?

Approvals

Does the approval engine support a two-step flow with conditional routing based on request type and field values, and can approvers be queues or roles rather than named individuals?

Intake form

Can intake be a guided form with required fields that change by request type, and can one request carry multiple part-number lines instead of an attached spreadsheet?

Queues and reporting

Can requests route to the Pricing Operations team distribution as a queue, with dashboards for volume, aging, and cycle time out of the box?

Audit trail

Is field history tracking available on this object, and would the resulting record satisfy a SOX walkthrough of a price or catalog change?

Requester access

Do product managers, pillar leads, and sales hold licenses that allow them to submit and track requests, or is there a licensing gap to plan for?

Build ownership and timeline

Who would build this, where does it sit in the current queue, and what is a realistic first-version timeline if we arrive with tested requirements?

Future extension

Is there anything in a v1 design on the ALI instance that would block extending to the SIG and Projectmates instances later, or adding an automated trigger from opportunity stage changes?

Definition of done for the handoff

  • The queue is live in Salesforce and Pricing Operations is triaging from it daily.
  • The two legacy forms are retired and the generic inbox auto-replies with the intake link.
  • Every request since go-live has a complete timestamped history from submission to load.
  • The first monthly operating report exists: volume by type, cycle time, aging, rejection rate.
  • The decision log entries flow into the Pricing SharePoint once it is live.
